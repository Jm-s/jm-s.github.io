date: 2026-09-08

A 2026 *Lancet Neurology* GWAS examined 1,573 AQP4-positive NMOSD cases and 1,260 controls.

### 1. Three independent signals

[![Figure 1: Sample distribution and pan-ancestry and European GWAS results]({{ site.url }}/assets/nmosd-genetics-2026/figure-1.webp)]({{ site.url }}/assets/nmosd-genetics-2026/figure-1.webp)

**Figure 1.** Two MHC signals and one STAT4 signal reached genome-wide significance. The strongest signal was associated with reduced C4, suggesting impaired B-cell tolerance.

### 2. The HLA region

[![Figure 2: European extended-MHC analysis and sequential conditional analyses]({{ site.url }}/assets/nmosd-genetics-2026/figure-2.webp)]({{ site.url }}/assets/nmosd-genetics-2026/figure-2.webp)

**Figure 2.** Conditional analyses separate two MHC signals. HLA-DRB1*03:01 and peptide-binding residues implicate antigen presentation, although linkage disequilibrium complicates causal attribution.

### 3. Shared autoimmune susceptibility

[![Figure 3: Shared genetic-risk network across autoimmune diseases]({{ site.url }}/assets/nmosd-genetics-2026/figure-3.webp)]({{ site.url }}/assets/nmosd-genetics-2026/figure-3.webp)

**Figure 3.** NMOSD shares susceptibility with systemic autoimmune diseases, including SLE and Sjögren’s syndrome. Thicker connections indicate greater overlap in associated variants, not clinical similarity.

**Takeaway:** These findings implicate tolerance failure and T-cell–B-cell cooperation. TYK2–STAT4 is a potential therapeutic target; treatment efficacy was not tested.
