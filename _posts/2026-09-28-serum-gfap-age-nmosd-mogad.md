title: "Serum GFAP and Age in NMOSD–MOGAD Differentiation"
date: 2026-09-28T09:36:21+09:00
---

**Serum GFAP combined with age showed strong discrimination between AQP4-IgG-positive NMOSD and MOGAD after a recent attack. The findings support further evaluation as an adjunct to antibody testing, with particular uncertainty around double-seronegative NMOSD.**

Rival and colleagues examined whether serum markers of astrocytic and neuroaxonal injury could help distinguish overlapping inflammatory CNS disorders.[1]

### Key findings

- **AQP4-NMOSD vs MOGAD:** age + sGFAP achieved an optimism-corrected AUC of **0.939**.
- **Double-seronegative NMOSD (DN-NMOSD):** elevated GFAP suggested astrocytic injury, but the recent-attack subgroup included only **8 patients**.
- **Clinical readiness:** the models underwent internal validation; external validation and evidence of clinical benefit are still needed.

### 1. Study design

This exploratory retrospective analysis used stored serum from the French **OFSEP cohort**, with patients enrolled in 2014–2018 and followed prospectively for at least 24 months.

| Feature | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Total cohort | 640 patients |
| AQP4-NMOSD / DN-NMOSD / MOGAD | 88 / 28 / 61 |
| CIS / RRMS / PPMS | 64 / 237 / 162 |
| Recent attack | Symptom onset ≤90 days before sampling |
| Recent-attack subgroup | 240 patients |
| Biomarkers | Serum GFAP and neurofilament light chain (NfL) |
| Assay | Simoa HD-1, Neurology 4-Plex A |
| Internal validation | 1,000 bootstrap resamples |

DN-NMOSD cases met the **2015 IPND criteria** and underwent expert review. AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG were assessed by live cell-based assays at least once.

GFAP reflects astrocytic injury, whereas NfL reflects neuroaxonal injury. **Measuring serum GFAP protein is distinct from testing for GFAP autoantibodies**; the latter was not performed.

### 2. Biomarker levels after a recent attack

| Group | n | sGFAP, pg/mL | sNfL, pg/mL |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| AQP4-NMOSD | 21 | 215.9 [162.4–635.3] | 34.1 [21.9–103.2] |
| DN-NMOSD | 8 | 175.8 [66.4–484.9] | 57.7 [8.5–312.7] |
| MOGAD | 19 | 71.1 [42.6–153.3] | 13.6 [7.9–36.6] |
| CIS | 34 | 64.3 [44.0–85.9] | 9.3 [6.3–14.2] |
| RRMS | 158 | 74.6 [56.3–105.9] | 11.5 [7.3–20.5] |

*Median [IQR]; selected data from Table 2.[1] These distributions are not diagnostic thresholds.*

GFAP was significantly higher in AQP4-NMOSD than in MOGAD, CIS, or RRMS. In AQP4-NMOSD, both biomarkers were higher after recent attacks than during stable disease.

DN-NMOSD showed numerically higher levels after attacks, but differences from stable disease were not significant: **GFAP p=0.3; NfL p=0.2**. These were comparisons between patient groups rather than paired changes within individuals.

### 3. What does age add?

The final multivariable models included **age and log(sGFAP)**. NfL was informative in univariable analyses but was not retained in the final models.

| Diagnostic comparison | Corrected AUC [95% CI] |
| --- | --- |
| AQP4-NMOSD vs MOGAD | **0.939 [0.910–0.982]** |
| AQP4-NMOSD vs DN-NMOSD/MOGAD/RRMS/CIS | 0.950 [0.918–0.987] |
| AQP4-NMOSD + DN-NMOSD vs MOGAD | 0.863 [0.776–0.977] |

*Recent-attack analyses; optimism-corrected estimates from Table 3.[1] PPMS was excluded from these comparisons.*

An AUC of 0.939 describes discrimination; it does **not** mean that 93.9% of patients were correctly diagnosed. Combining DN-NMOSD with AQP4-NMOSD reduced discrimination from MOGAD, and does not establish performance for DN-NMOSD alone.

[Original Figure 3 — age–GFAP probability heatmaps](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Probability-heatmaps-of-belonging-to-one-diagnostic-group-against-other-groups_fig3_414819586)

**Reading the figure:** age changes the estimated probability at a given GFAP concentration. Those probabilities depend on the study population and require validation before use in other clinical settings.

### 4. What can we infer about DN-NMOSD?

Across the full cohort, GFAP was higher in AQP4-NMOSD than in DN-NMOSD: **145.5 vs 92.3 pg/mL, p=0.005**. Within the recent-attack subgroup, univariable measures did not significantly distinguish the two groups. GFAP was also associated with disability in DN-NMOSD.

These observations are compatible with astrocytic injury in at least some DN-NMOSD cases. **Similar injury markers do not establish shared antibodies, complement activation, or T-cell mechanisms.**

| Question | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence of astrocytic injury? | A plausible signal in this cohort |
| Same pathogenesis as AQP4-NMOSD? | Not established |
| A biologically uniform DN-NMOSD entity? | Not established |
| Distinct immune-cell or cytokine profiles? | Not investigated |
| Validated DN-NMOSD vs MOGAD classifier? | Not demonstrated independently |

With only eight recent-attack DN-NMOSD cases, a nonsignificant difference cannot establish equivalence. Differences from earlier reports may reflect disease heterogeneity or sampling time. GFAP astrocytopathy was not specifically excluded by antibody testing.

### 5. Clinical interpretation and limitations

Age and GFAP may eventually support differential diagnosis while antibody results are pending. This study did not test biomarker-guided treatment or demonstrate improved outcomes.

- **Sampling window:** ≤90 days is broad; median sampling times in the main disease groups were approximately 22–41 days. Performance at emergency presentation remains uncertain.
- **Generalizability:** validation was internal only.
- **Confounding:** BMI and renal function were unavailable; lesion burden and treatment effects were incompletely addressed.
- **Statistical uncertainty:** small subgroups, exploratory variable selection, and no correction for multiple comparisons.
- **Treatment decisions:** no validated GFAP-based rule for initiating plasma exchange or withholding treatment was established.

**Take-home message:** age-adjusted sGFAP is a promising adjunct for distinguishing AQP4-NMOSD from MOGAD after an attack. The DN-NMOSD findings warrant further study of astrocytic injury and disease heterogeneity.
