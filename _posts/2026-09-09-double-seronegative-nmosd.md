title: Double-seronegative NMOSD — how different is its immune biology?
date: 2026-09-09
---

**Yes—direct comparative studies report biological differences from AQP4-positive NMOSD and, in proteomic analyses, from MOGAD. However, they do not yet establish one uniform double-seronegative disease.**

### 1. The 2026 review: clinical findings

Malvaso et al. pooled **41 studies involving 671 double-negative patients**.[1]

- **Relapsing course:** 73.6% in the included cohorts.
- **Baseline ARR:** 1.08 versus 0.84 in AQP4-positive NMOSD and 0.61 in NMOSD-like MOGAD; the group difference was **not significant (p = 0.08)**.
- **Maintenance immunosuppression:** before/after rate ratio **0.19 (95% CI 0.07–0.49)**, with substantial heterogeneity (**I² = 90%**).
- **Interpretation:** an observational treatment signal, not proof of an 81% causal benefit. The pooled relapse proportion is not a first-attack patient's prospective relapse probability.

**This is clinical evidence. The mechanistic question requires separate biomarker and immune-profile studies.**

### 2. Tissue injury: less evidence of AQP4-like astrocyte damage

**GFAP measures astrocytic injury; it is not an immune-cell profile or an antigen-specific test.**

| Direct comparison | Main result | What it supports |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **CSF GFAP:** 17 double-negative, 30 AQP4-positive, 17 MOGAD; samples within one month of an attack.[2] | Median **0.49 vs 102.9 vs 0.39 ng/mL**, respectively. Double-negative levels were lower than AQP4-positive levels and similar to MOGAD and neurological controls. | The sampled double-negative group lacked the marked astrocytic-injury signal seen in AQP4-positive disease. |
| **Serum biomarkers:** 26 double-negative vs 25 AQP4-positive; samples within three months of onset/relapse.[3] | GFAP **103.4 vs 308.3 pg/mL (p = 0.001)**. Tau and UCH-L1 were also lower; **NfL did not differ significantly (p = 0.113)**. | A second compartment supports a different injury profile, despite overlapping clinical presentations. |

- **Why the CSF study matters:** antibody negativity was checked repeatedly with cell-based assays, including during exacerbation; this makes a simple testing explanation less persuasive.[2]
- **Limit:** low GFAP does not exclude every form of astrocyte involvement. An earlier three-patient series reported elevations in two double-negative patients, illustrating uncertainty across cohorts.[2]
- **Do not infer:** similar GFAP in double-negative NMOSD and MOGAD means identical pathogenesis.

### 3. Immune profiles: direct evidence beyond antibody negativity

**A. Serum proteomics — all three disease groups compared**

Gawde et al. measured **1,463 proteins** in 53 AQP4-positive, 25 MOGAD, 18 seronegative NMOSD patients and 49 healthy controls. Disease signatures were examined in the subset without anti-CD20 B-cell depletion.[4]

| Group | Reported signature |
| --- | --- |
| **AQP4-positive NMOSD** | Chemokines **CXCL9, CXCL10 and CXCL13** |
| **MOGAD** | Cytotoxic-cell-associated proteases **granzyme B and granzyme H** |
| **Seronegative NMOSD** | **Wnt inhibitory factor 1 (WIF1)**, interpreted as an injury-associated signal |

- **Meaning:** the study found measurable differences beyond the diagnostic antibodies.
- **Limit:** retrospective discovery analysis, with small disease subgroups; WIF1 is not a validated disease-defining biomarker.
- **Treatment caveat:** comparisons between B-cell-depleted and non-depleted patients are not randomized treatment effects. “No anti-CD20 treatment” does not necessarily mean untreated.

**B. Peripheral immune cells — double-negative vs AQP4-positive**

A small study compared **8 double-negative and 10 AQP4-positive patients**, alongside MS and healthy controls. Participants were in remission and had received no immunomodulatory/immunosuppressive medication during the preceding three months.[5]

- **Peripheral helper T cells (CXCR5−PD1+):** higher in double-negative patients than in all comparator groups.
- **CD8+ T cells:** higher than in AQP4-positive NMOSD and MS, **but not significantly higher than healthy controls**.
- **Th17 cells, plasmablasts and total B-cell frequencies:** no significant group differences.
- **Meaning:** a preliminary immune-cell distinction; **not proof of a predominantly T-cell-mediated disease**.
- **Limit:** only eight double-negative cases and **no MOGAD comparator**.

### 4. How strong is the case for a separate disease?

| Question | Evidence-based assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **Different from typical AQP4-positive astrocytopathy?** | **Supported at the group level**, particularly by the CSF and serum injury-marker studies.[2,3] |
| **A distinct immune profile?** | **Suggestive evidence** from proteomics and peripheral T cells; replication is needed.[4,5] |
| **Different from MOGAD?** | Proteomic differences are reported, but low GFAP alone cannot distinguish them.[2,4] |
| **One coherent disease shared by all double-negative patients?** | **Not established.** These studies do not identify a shared causal antigen or mechanism. |
| **Simply false-negative AQP4/MOG testing?** | This may explain some cases; rigorously retested cohorts argue against assuming it explains every case.[2] |

*These assessments are a synthesis of the studies, not a formal evidence-grading system.*

### Final takeaways

1. **There is positive biological evidence of differences—not just absence of antibodies.**
2. **Evidence for separation from typical AQP4-positive disease is stronger than evidence that all double-negative patients share one disease.**
3. **My interpretation: retain double-seronegative NMOSD as a carefully defined clinical category, while investigating biologically distinct subgroups.** Neither a low GFAP value nor a research immune signature can currently determine an individual patient's mechanism.
