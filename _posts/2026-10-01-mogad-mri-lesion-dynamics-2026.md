### Key findings

- MRI abnormalities can lag behind symptoms.
- T2 lesions often resolve, unlike the persistent lesions typical of MS.
- Silent radiological activity is uncommon; surveillance MRI has less utility than in MS.

### Interpretation and limitations

**Editorial interpretation:** recording symptom onset and the purpose of each scan may be as important as recording the scan date. A useful report should make clear whether it describes the acute episode, recovery or a possible new episode.

This summary cannot specify the recommended acquisition protocol or rebaselining interval. Lower surveillance yield does not establish that routine MRI should always be abandoned. Likewise, disappearance of a lesion does not by itself establish full functional recovery. Those are clinical interpretation cautions, not additional results from this review.

### Take-home message

Interpret MOGAD MRI longitudinally. The meaning of a scan depends on when and why it was obtained.
